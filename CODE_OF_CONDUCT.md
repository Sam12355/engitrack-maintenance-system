# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to do good work. Criticise the code, not the person who
wrote it.

## What is expected

- Give feedback on the work. "This check runs in the guard, so ownership is not enforced" is useful.
  "This is sloppy" is not.
- Assume good faith. Most bad code is someone doing their best with incomplete context, under time
  pressure, in an area they did not know well.
- Say when you do not know something. A team where nobody admits that is a team that ships bugs
  nobody wanted to ask about.
- Accept that a reviewer who blocks your change is doing their job, and that a reviewer who approves
  everything is not.
- Keep discussion in the open where it belongs, so decisions have a record.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, discrimination, or personal attacks of any kind
- Unwelcome sexual attention
- Publishing someone's private information
- Sustained disruption of discussion
- Dismissing someone's contribution on the basis of who they are rather than what they wrote

## Scope

Applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code comments, and any space representing this
project.

## Reporting

Open an issue, or contact the maintainer directly if it is not appropriate in public. Reports are
handled confidentially. This is a portfolio repository with one maintainer, so there is no committee
and no formal process; there is just someone who will read it and act on it.

## Enforcement

Warning, then temporary block, then permanent block, depending on severity and on whether it happens
again. A single serious incident does not need to escalate through the earlier steps.
